The program

What you'll do with us

Vector pairs high school students with mentorship, learning material, and a clear path through a real research project.

Who it's for

High school students in any grade, with or without a science or coding background.

What it costs

Nothing. There's no tuition, no materials fee, and nothing to buy to take part.

What you do

Pick a question, learn the methods, run the study, and present it at competitions.

Outcomes

What you leave with

  • A research question that is yours, not assigned
  • Working skills in the methods your project used
  • A written paper and a competition-ready poster
  • Practice presenting and answering judges' questions
  • A project you can talk about in any interview

How to use them

Run the code

Every code example runs in Google Colab, free in your browser. Type it out the first time; you'll remember it.

Answer the checks

Each chapter ends with a few questions. If you can answer them without looking back, move on.

Bring questions to your mentor

Mark anything confusing and bring it to your next meeting. Your mentor can tell you which chapters your project needs first.

About

Why Vector exists

A vector has a direction and a magnitude. We help students choose the direction and give them what they need to go far with it.

Most high schoolers never get near real research. The ones who do usually had a way in. We're building a way in for everyone else.

Equal access

Students shouldn't need a parent in a lab, a paid program, or a famous school to do research. Vector is free and open to every high school student.

Real research

Students work on original questions and finish with something they can present and defend. No worksheets dressed up as projects.

Learning by doing

You learn machine learning and scientific methods because your project needs them, which is the fastest way they stick.

Mentors who stay

Guidance continues from the first brainstorm to the last question from a judge.

Questions

Questions from students and parents

For students

Do I need to know how to code?

No. The Python for Machine Learning guide starts from your first line of code, and your mentor helps you learn only what your project needs.

What fields can I research?

Projects lean toward computational science and machine learning, applied to areas like biology, medicine, and physics. If you have an idea outside that, tell us in your application.

Which competitions do students enter?

Regional and state science fairs, plus national research competitions. Your mentor helps you choose ones that fit your project and timeline.

Will I get published?

We can't promise it. Publication depends on the work and the journal. We'll help you write a paper that's ready to submit if your results support it.

For parents and teachers

Is it really free?

Yes. Vector Research is a nonprofit and doesn't charge students for anything it provides. Some outside competitions charge their own entry fees, and we help students find free ones and fee waivers.

How do I contact you?

Email contact@vectorresearch.org. Parents, teachers, and counselors are all welcome to write.

I'm a teacher. How can I share this?

Share this page with any curious student, or email us and we'll send a short description you can pass along.
